An audit of 9 AI-text detectors · 15,282 essays

Fixed by AI,
flagged as AI.

Let an AI tool fix the grammar of an essay you wrote, and AI-text detectors start calling it AI. I am applying to US universities in my second language, so I tested nine detectors on 15,282 essays. They are not biased against English learners, as a well-known 2023 study suggested. They are biased against text that is short, formulaic, or touched by a language model.

Writing quality

Do better essays get flagged more?

Trained raters scored every essay from 1 to 6. It depends on the detector: Fakespot and SuperAnnotate flag the strongest native essays most, Desklib does the opposite. For English learners, eight of nine detectors rate more fluent essays as more AI-like.

What to do with this

If a detector is part of your process

Students

Proofread with a plain spellchecker. Accepting an AI tool's grammar fixes on an essay you wrote yourself can get it flagged as AI.

Keep drafts and version history. They show your process better than any detector score.

Teachers

Do not act on a flag alone. Short answers and formulaic test essays are flagged far more often than full-length essays.

Before using a detector, check its false-positive rate on writing from your own students.

Admissions readers

Polished application essays are the texts these tools misread most: published model essays were flagged at up to 83%.

An applicant writing in a second language is not more likely to be flagged. An applicant who used an AI grammar fixer is.

Method

How this was done

Data

PERSUADE 2.0: argumentative essays by US students in grades 6–12 on 15 prompts, written before ChatGPT, with English-learner status and a 1–6 score from trained raters. ELLIPSE: essays by English learners with 1–5 proficiency ratings. Liang et al. (2023): their 91 TOEFL essays and 70 published US college-admission essays. DAIGT v2: 2,989 essays by ChatGPT, GPT-4, Claude, PaLM, Llama 2, Mistral and others on the same prompts.

Detectors

Six trained classifiers (OpenAI's 2019 GPT-2 detector, HC3, RADAR, SuperAnnotate, Fakespot/Mozilla, Desklib) and three zero-shot scores (perplexity, Binoculars, Fast-DetectGPT), all run locally. Commercial detectors such as Turnitin, GPTZero and Pangram are not included.

Edits

AI grammar fix: an open model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) told to correct only spelling, grammar and punctuation and change nothing else; it kept 81–93% of words in place. Spellcheck: a dictionary spellchecker fixing misspelled lowercase words only. Length: essays cut to the length of the TOEFL answers.

Limitations

Open detectors only. The English learners in PERSUADE are learning English in US schools, which is not the same as applying from abroad. The published college essays are polished examples, not a random sample. The zero-shot detectors use smaller models than their original papers.
